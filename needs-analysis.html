CHousehold

Under the Income Tax Act, that person must be the survivor spouse (legally married) or common-law partner: living in a conjugal relationship for at least 12 continuous months, or the parents of a child together by birth or adoption, or with custody and control of a wholly dependent child. For a TFSA, they must be named successor holder in the contract or will where provincial law allows the designation. For an RRSP or RRIF, they must be the designated beneficiary (or RRIF successor annuitant) so the amount can transfer to their own registered plan on a tax-deferred basis. Checking this assumes those designations are in place. It is not a tax opinion.

GTax and probate on death

Canada has no inheritance tax. Probate is a court fee on what actually goes through the estate. A non-registered account, and other assets that move through the estate, can be taxed as a capital gain on death. An RRSP is income on the final return unless it rolls to an eligible beneficiary. Those taxes are added to the life insurance need, and the chart raises them as the accounts and the estate value grow. This is a screen, not a tax opinion.

HAssumptions you can change for this case

These are planning choices for this case. Inflation is the slider in the retirement section. Life insurance capital stays in today's dollars, so that slider does not shrink the insurance number.

In retirement, CPP and OAS (when they apply) cover part of the after-tax income goal. Any shortfall is taken from Other assets, non-registered accounts, and the RRSP in proportion to those three balances, so they run down together and reach $0 at the same time. Example: if those accounts total $1,000,000 — $500,000 Other, $300,000 non-registered, and $200,000 RRSP — a $100,000 shortfall draws about $50,000, $30,000, and $20,000 from each. The TFSA is not used until Other, non-registered, and the RRSP are gone.
