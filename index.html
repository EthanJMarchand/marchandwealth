What I Help With

Planning built around your real financial picture

Every recommendation is weighed against your full picture — your income and your family, and, if you're incorporated, your company's balance sheet too.

01

Life & Income Protection

Life insurance, critical illness, and disability coverage built around your actual income, debts, and family — whether or not a business is involved. The goal is a straightforward answer to what happens to the people who depend on you if something happens to you.

02

Corporate Insurance Strategy

Life and critical illness coverage structured to fund buy-sell agreements, protect against the loss of a key person, and move surplus corporate cash tax-efficiently rather than leaving it exposed to passive income tax inside the company. We look at how coverage is owned, who's named as beneficiary, and how the capital dividend account is affected before any policy is put in place.

03

Personal Wealth & Estate Planning

Coordinating your personal assets — and corporate holdings, if you have them — so more of what you've built reaches your family instead of the tax bill. This includes reviewing how your estate will be taxed on death, whether probate can be reduced, and how personally-held versus corporately-held insurance changes the outcome for your beneficiaries.

04

Succession & Legacy Planning

For owners planning to pass the business on rather than sell it: structuring ownership transitions across generations, funding buy-sell agreements between family members or partners, and making sure the next generation inherits a business that's actually set up to be run, not just owned.

Yes. Key person insurance protects the business from the financial impact of losing an owner or essential employee, but it typically pays out to the corporation, not your family. Personal life insurance is a separate layer that protects your household's income, debts, and long-term goals directly.

When a corporation owns a life insurance policy, the death benefit (less the policy's adjusted cost basis) can generally be credited to the company's capital dividend account, allowing that amount to be paid to shareholders tax-free. It also allows surplus corporate cash to grow inside an exempt policy rather than being taxed annually as passive investment income.

A buy-sell agreement is a legal contract that determines what happens to a business owner's share of the company if they die, become disabled, or exit. Funding that agreement with life insurance ensures the remaining owners have the capital to buy out a departing owner's share without straining the business or forcing a sale.

It depends on personal income replacement needs, outstanding debt, business valuation, buy-sell obligations, and estate tax exposure. Most business owners need coverage calculated separately for personal protection and for business purposes, since the two serve different goals.

Personally-owned insurance is paid for with after-tax personal dollars and pays out directly to your named beneficiaries. Corporately-owned insurance is paid for with corporate dollars, which can be more tax-efficient for business owners, but the ownership, beneficiary designation, and tax treatment need to be structured carefully.
